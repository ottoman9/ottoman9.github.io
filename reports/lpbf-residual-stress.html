L-PBF Residual Stress in IN625: a Melt-Pool Calibration That Holds at One Point, and Three Negatives With Mechanisms

Author: Usman Bashir, BSc Metallurgical and Materials Engineering Date: 2026-08-31 Solver: Abaqus 2024/Standard, 4 cores

Internal working records cited in this report (for example DECISIONS, RESULTS_LOG and the sources ledger) are available on request.

A note on how to read this. This report contains a retraction. A result this project logged as a pass was withdrawn by its own audit, and the sections below present it as such rather than quietly omitting it. Section 8 lists every disclosure that would be invisible from the plots.

0. Executive summary

Laser powder bed fusion leaves a part in tension. Cut it off the plate and it springs. NIST AM Bench 2018 published the machine settings, the geometry, and the measured spring for an IN625 bridge: 1.276 mm upward at ridge 1 after a partial wire-EDM cut. This project asked whether a 4-core Abaqus model, with nothing tuned against that number, could land inside a pre-declared plus or minus 25 percent band.

It could not, and the interesting part is why.

M1, melt pool. Absorptivity was calibrated on one case only, the AMB2018-01 infill condition (195 W, 800 mm/s), and frozen at eta = 0.66. That case passes: solidus width and depth 144.6 and 88.6 um against measured 133 and 91 um. Both holdouts miss, and the misses are monotonic in scan speed: too shallow at 400 mm/s, too large at 1200 mm/s. A counterfactual shows the misses are structural rather than a mis-set eta: rescuing cmb_a would need an absorptivity of 1.69, above unity, and cmb_c would need 0.19, below the declared floor. M1 is a single-point calibration, not a validated process window (Figures 1 and 2).

M2 thermal. The bridge was voxelized from the NIST STL to within 0.04 percent of its exact volume. Only three super-layer thicknesses land on the part's own z planes, so the lumping ladder has two admissible rungs, not three. Both solved. The activation artifact, measured in the real configuration, is 0.64 percent coarse and 0.67 percent fine against a before-the-fact prediction of about 0.6 percent. The lumped history peaks well below the solidus and therefore contains no millimetre-scale eigenstrain.

M2 deflection: four attempts, item 2 closed as a MISS (Figure 3).

Attempt 1 landed inside the band and was withdrawn on internal audit: it had zero plasticity (0 of 238,976 integration points yielded), an inert EDM step that removed nothing and was nonetheless graded against a post-cut measurement, and its constraint condition had been selected after seeing the answer.

Phase B-2, scan strategy: FAIL (Figure 5). The production-tool method (path-aware anisotropic inherent strain) separates the strategies by a factor of two, but chessboard is third of four rather than worst, and the strip class occupies both extremes.

Phase C, compensation: workflow demonstration only (Figure 6). The 95.4 percent reduction was produced on the retracted elastic model, where inverting and reapplying a displacement field is guaranteed by superposition.

The substantive contribution is three negatives with mechanisms and citations, and one calibration that holds where it was calibrated.

1. The problem

In L-PBF a 20 um layer is melted, frozen, and reheated by every later pass. Hot metal wants to shrink; colder metal below will not let it. The stored tension is residual stress, and cutting the part off the plate releases it as a spring, which is the quantity a shop pays for.

AM Bench 2018 makes this falsifiable: published process parameters, published geometry, and a measured 1.276 mm.

2. Method and discipline

Two rules did the real work, and both are why the numbers below can be trusted more than a clean-looking result would be.

Every number traces to a ledger row or is a labelled assumption with its bias direction stated. Where a value could not be traced, the route is recorded as closed rather than filled in.

Declare before solving. Configurations, bands and quantitative predictions were written down before runs, so outcomes confirm or refute a stated expectation. Two of those predictions failed and are reported as failed (Section 8.9). That is the evidence the pre-registrations were real.

3. M1: melt pool, and where a frozen source stops working

Goldak double ellipsoid via a DFLUX subroutine, IN625 properties from Lim et al. Absorptivity calibrated on cmb_b only, then frozen.

Case Process Measured W/D um Predicted W/D um Verdict
cmb_a 150 W, 400 mm/s 171 / 151 176.1 / 107.5 MISS, depth -28.8 pct
cmb_b 195 W, 800 mm/s 133 / 91 144.6 / 88.6 PASS
cmb_c 195 W, 1200 mm/s 100 / 60 125.7 / 83.2 MISS, +25.7 and +38.7 pct

The mechanism. The source depth semi-axis was frozen at cmb_b's measured depth, which pins the predicted depth-to-width ratio between 0.61 and 0.66 for every case, while the measured ratios span 0.600 to 0.883. Absorptivity scales a pool but cannot change its aspect ratio.

Two findings from the calibration itself. Fitting the measured sensitivity gives dimension proportional to eta to the power 0.187 for width and 0.362 for depth, far below the 0.5 assumed. So eta is a weak knob: across the entire declared range 0.30 to 1.00 the predicted width moves only 25 percent. Inverting that, the plus or minus 10 percent band could have been satisfied by any eta from 0.49 to 0.86. The honest statement is not eta = 0.66 but eta near 0.5 to 0.86 with 0.66 as the centre, and any downstream quantity sensitive to absorbed energy inherits that spread.

4. M2 thermal, and an audit that failed on purpose

The bridge (75.000 x 5.000 x 12.500 mm from the STL, note the ledger said 12 mm) was voxelized on a 0.5 mm lattice that reproduces every axis-aligned feature exactly and staircases only the 45 degree leg overhangs. Voxel volume 3734.0 mm3 against an exact 3732.5, an error of +0.04 percent in the direction predicted beforehand.

Absorbed energy enters as eta times power over the real scan time, so the frozen eta is the only thing M2 inherits from M1.

Activation is a declared parameter, because it had to be measured. Abaqus does not activate elements at their initial condition: an isolated zero-flux experiment showed newly activated nodes are raised to the neighbouring field temperature, creating 88 percent of the domain enthalpy from nothing. New material now enters at a declared 298 K. The residual artifact, measured in the real configuration, is 0.64 and 0.67 percent for the two rungs.

C3 item 6 FAILS and stays failing. Re-derived with a corrected integrator, M1 closes at 1.956, 1.953 and 1.869 percent against a 1 percent threshold. Two of those verdicts flipped from PASS when the auditor was fixed, and the original figures are preserved in the log. The auditor was cleared by self-test to 1e-12 and both auditor-side explanations were eliminated; the remaining candidate is solver time integration across the latent heat band, logged as open and quantified rather than guessed at.

5. M2 deflection: the retraction, and three honest attempts

5.1 Sequential coupling of the lumped thermal

The brief's default. It maxes out near 0.2 mm and can point the wrong way. The reason is physical: the first lumped layer on the plate sink peaks at 313 K and the fine rung peaks at 907.6 K, never reaching the 1563 K solidus. That history does not contain a millimetre-scale spring, so no amount of mechanical post-processing can produce one.

5.2 The retracted result

An inherent-strain model was built with a stress-free linear temperature gradient running from ambient at the plate to the solidus at the top, cooled under a three-point locator. It gave +1.115 mm coarse and +1.166 mm fine, inside the band, and was logged as a PASS.

It was withdrawn by internal audit. Read from the results file rather than the log:

  • Zero plasticity. 0 of 238,976 integration points yielded, peak Mises 402.5 MPa against a 479.2 MPa yield. The model is elastic. The yield card, and the multi-day search behind it, do not touch the number at all.
  • No cut was modelled. The step named EDM re-declared identical boundary conditions and removed no elements. Displacements and stresses are identical before and after it. The output was labelled "after EDM" and graded against a post-cut measurement.
  • The constraint was chosen after seeing the answer. The glued sequence, which is what a part built on a plate actually experiences, gave 0.11 mm. Removing the glue gave 1.115 mm. The zero-plasticity finding explains the factor of ten: the glued model yields and relaxes, the free model never yields.

Item 3 (convergence) and item 5 (compensation) are downgraded with it, because both sit on that model.

5.3 Two traced attempts, and why they miss

Both used published, calibrated inherent-strain components (-0.011 longitudinal, -0.007 transverse, 0 build direction), the benchmark's own scan strategy for orientation, the part welded to the plate while it contracts, and a genuinely modelled release. One applied the strain in one shot (+0.0709 mm), the other layer by layer per the published method (-0.0600 mm). Both miss by more than an order of magnitude.

The mechanism. The traced eigenstrain is about five times the yield strain of this material card (479.2 MPa over 207.5 GPa). Plastic flow accommodates it rather than storing it: 72 percent of the model yielded in the first, 99.9 percent in the second. Almost no elastic energy survives to be released.

5.4 The fourth attempt, stopped at the reading stage

A full method transplant was authorised, conditional on first reading the source method end to end and reproducing its own validation case. Reading it stopped the attempt, for three reasons:

  1. The eigenstrain values are a fit, not material constants. They were obtained by Newton-Raphson optimisation against the authors' own measured deflection until relative error fell below 0.5 percent, on a cantilever coupon, with that software's flow-stress data, their mesh, and their lumping of 50 modelled layers standing in for 625 physical ones.
  2. The constitutive core is unpublished. Their plasticity is mechanism-based (dislocation interaction, Hall-Petch at 20 um grain size, solid solution strengthening) with parameters stated to come from prior work and not given.
  3. The validation geometry is unpublished, so the reproduction gate that would have proven the implementation cannot be run.

Two citations from that reading convert this from our failure into a statement about the method. The source applies its eigenstrain against a deliberately raised virgin yield, because the inherent strain method does not account for process-induced hardening. And even so, calibrated on its own coupon, it underpredicts its own bridge by 27 and 18 percent, while its best thermo-mechanical model still underpredicts by 10 to 19 percent.

5.5 Attribution: is it our material card?

A yield sensitivity sweep was run as an attribution axis, explicitly not graded and explicitly not a fifth attempt (Figure 4). Across the converged range, 479.2 MPa (annealed wrought) to 600 MPa, the spring stays at or below 0.07 mm with plasticity near total. The material card does not explain the miss.

Stated with its defects, always: two of five traced points (660 and 720 MPa) did not converge, the response is non-monotonic and flips sign at 600 MPa, and the non-physical 5000 MPa bracket reached a plastic strain of 2.21 and -7.58 mm and is withdrawn as unusable.

6. Phase B-2: scan strategy ranking, FAIL

Path-aware anisotropic inherent strain, the method production tools use for this question. Strategy changes the per-element orientation field and nothing else.

Strategy change across release post-cut rise
strip_X, along the length +0.0311 mm +0.0779 mm
chess, 5 mm islands +0.0577 mm +0.0966 mm
strip_Y, across the width +0.0586 mm +0.0881 mm
strip_XY, 90/90 +0.0653 mm +0.1032 mm

The gate requires chessboard worst and strip-class least. Chessboard is third of four, and the strip class occupies both extremes. FAIL.

Two reportable reasons. Orientation alone does not carry what makes a chessboard bad, namely island boundaries, short vectors and start-stop effects, which was pre-registered and held. And grouping by "strip class" is not meaningful under this model, because strip direction relative to the part axis dominates.

This is a sharper failure than the previous attempt, where three strategies clustered within 0.01 mm and told us nothing. Here they separate by a factor of two and the ranking still does not match.

7. Phase C: compensation, workflow demonstration only

Ridge-1 deviation fell from +1.1151 mm to +0.0514 mm, a 95.4 percent reduction. This was produced on the retracted elastic model, and in a linear elastic system inverting a displacement field and reapplying it is guaranteed by superposition. It demonstrates that the compensation workflow is wired correctly. It is not evidence about compensation physics, and the caveat belongs in the same sentence as the number wherever it appears.

8. Disclosures

  1. An initially logged PASS was retracted on internal audit. C3 item 2 was reported as passing at +1.115 and +1.166 mm. It was withdrawn for zero plasticity, an inert EDM step, and outcome-based selection of the constraint. Items 3 and 5 are downgraded with it.
  2. C3 item 8 was revised after an observed failure. The peak temperature ceiling moved from 5000 K to a 20000 K screen once the original was shown to fail at every physical eta. This is a gate changed after a failure and is disclosed as such.
  3. C3 item 6 fails everywhere. M1 at 1.87 to 1.96 percent against a 1 percent threshold, cause not decomposed. M2 globally, with the usable contamination number being the 0.64 and 0.67 percent activation artifact.
  4. M1 is a single-point calibration, not a validated process window, and eta is only pinned to roughly 0.5 to 0.86.
  5. The benchmark does not publish material properties. It supplies powder characterisation, process parameters, geometry and mill certificates only. Every modelling group brings its own card, which is why ours is ours.
  6. The yield curve is a digitised figure, wrought annealed rather than as-built LPBF, carried as a labelled assumption with no bias direction asserted, because lower yield means more plastic flow while plastic flow also relieves the stress that drives springback, and that cannot be signed cleanly.
  7. Nothing is labelled "after EDM" unless a cut is modelled. The retracted model does not model one; attempts 2 and 3 do.
  8. The two thermal rungs differ by 0.44 percent of metal, because refining only in z staircases the overhangs differently. Flagged before any comparison, and it never became load-bearing.
  9. Two pre-registered predictions failed and are reported as failed. Layerwise application was predicted to yield less and spring more; it yielded more (99.9 percent) and sprang the wrong way. And scanning along the bridge's length was predicted to deflect most; it deflects least, because bending needs a through-thickness gradient rather than a large uniform strain.
  10. A declaration of mine was wrong and was corrected. Phase B-2 was declared as inheriting an elastic parent. It was built on the constrained sequence and yields heavily. The runs are the authority over the declaration.
  11. The yield sensitivity sweep is incomplete, non-monotonic, and its bracket run is withdrawn.

9. What this report may and may not claim

May claim. The M1 calibration at 195 W and 800 mm/s. The M1 speed trend and its aspect-ratio mechanism, including the counterfactual that no admissible absorptivity rescues either holdout. The measured activation convention and the artifact it leaves. That sequential coupling of a lumped thermal history cannot produce the NIST spring, with the reason. That a published inherent-strain calibration is a fit to one coupon, one card and one discretisation, and does not transfer, by the source's own evidence and ours. That within a traced yield range the material card does not explain the miss.

May not claim. A melt-pool model across the CBM process window. A deflection prediction against A2: item 2 is a MISS. A converged lumping ladder: item 3 is downgraded. A scan-strategy ranking: item 4 fails. Compensation physics: item 5 is a workflow demonstration. Eta as a sharp 0.66. Any result from the retracted model.
