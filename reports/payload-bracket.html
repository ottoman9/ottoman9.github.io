Payload Bracket for a 2 kg Avionics Box: Modal Decoupling, GEVS Random Vibration, 3-Sigma Margins and Steinberg Fatigue

Author: Usman Bashir, BSc Metallurgical and Materials Engineering Date: 2026-09-28 Solver: Abaqus/Standard 2024, C3D10 solids, cpus=2 (Phases 1, 2, 2b), except the M6 joint model at P_max, resubmitted at cpus=8

Internal working records cited in this report (for example DECISIONS, RESULTS_LOG and the sources ledger) are available on request.

Style: no em dash or en dash as sentence punctuation.

0. Executive summary

Self-contained. Every statement here is carried from a numbered section or from RESULTS_LOG.md.

A 2 kg avionics box bolted to a launch-vehicle panel by an L-bracket must survive the NASA GEVS random-vibration qualification spectrum (20 to 2000 Hz, 14.1 Grms, 2 min per axis) without its support structure resonating in the spectrum, and without its bolted base joint failing under the same 3-sigma loads. The bracket is aluminium 6061-T6. The baseline (web 6 mm, flange 6 mm) fails on frequency: its first mode is 91.08 Hz, below the declared 100 Hz requirement. The stiffened design (web 7 mm, flange 6 mm, no gussets, bracket V-A) raises the first mode to 101.47 Hz with the originally specified M5 base bolts, passing the frequency requirement, but a nonlinear-contact joint model of that design found two negative joint margins (bolt tension yield and slip, both at Q = 20). The design was then changed to four A286 160 ksi M6 bolts in 6.5 mm holes with 12 mm washers, the smallest fix in the declared order of levers. On the final design, V-A with M6 bolts, every continuum and joint margin is positive.

Headline numbers, final design (V-A, M6 bolts), 1.6 mm mesh unless noted, d = 3.0 mm base singularity zone, box-mount footprint excluded (d_box = 0).

  • First mode: 104.41 Hz at 1.6 mm (M6 geometry). 101.33 Hz is the converged value on the 1.2 mm mesh (M5 geometry, a lower bound for the stiffer M6 design). Requirement 100 Hz: PASS.
  • Box RMS accelerations at the centre of gravity, M6 geometry: Q = 10 (X 20.97, Y 22.57, Z 22.11 g), Q = 20 (X 28.86, Y 31.84, Z 30.00 g).
  • Worst continuum margin of safety: yield +0.59, ultimate +0.71, both at Q = 20, Y case, on the flange adjacent to the (excluded) box-mount footprint.
  • Fatigue outside the singular and excluded zones, M6 geometry, 4x life factor: 9.3e-6 at Q = 10, 9.0e-5 at Q = 20. Fatigue negligible.
  • Bolted joint, checked as a joint with a nonlinear contact FE model (preloaded bolts, flange-to-panel contact, finite washers): worst margins at Q = 20 are bolt tension yield +0.024, bolt tension ultimate +0.43, separation (NASA-STD-5020 full-relief definition) above +0.14, slip +0.075, shank shear +2.42, bearing +1.41. Every joint margin is positive.
  • V1b, an independent hand model with the flange-root rotational spring, confirms the mechanism behind V1's failure: +5.5 percent against FE on the final design, inside its 10 percent band. V1 itself stays FAIL, unchanged in the record (+50.6 percent); V1b cannot and does not turn it into a pass.
  • V2 (mesh convergence, 1.6 against 1.2 mm) is graded PASS: -0.14, -0.34, -0.23 percent on modes 1 to 3.
  • Bracket mass: 0.3671 kg with M6 base holes (0.3374 kg baseline), an increase of 0.0297 kg, +8.8 percent of the bracket and +1.3 percent of the total supported mass.

The validation chain confirms the random-response pipeline is trustworthy and that the declared failures are understood, not hidden. V4 validates the solver's random-response engine to within 0.20 percent against an exact integral. V1 fails by a growing margin because its closed form omits the flange-root rotational spring, and V1b, an independent hand model built specifically to include that spring, closes the gap to within its own 10 percent band without turning V1 itself into a pass. V5b fails because two modes share the box's effective mass in every axis, so Miles' equation on the largest single mode systematically overpredicts the box response. The first-mode requirement stays at 100 Hz as declared (R-1); the baseline fails it and there is no lowering of the goalposts.

1. The problem

An avionics box mounted on a spacecraft or launch-vehicle primary structure is subjected to random vibration during launch. The structure must transmit the acceleration profile from the base to the box without introducing new resonances in the input spectrum. For this application the requirement is set at a first natural frequency of 100 Hz or higher, to give a decoupling margin above the launch vehicle's own low-frequency modes and the GEVS baseline figures of 50 Hz lateral and 70 Hz thrust. The design question is: what bracket geometry and stiffness are necessary, and after stiffening does the bracket, including its bolted base joint, have positive stress margins and fatigue life to survive 2 minutes of qualification per axis?

Problem statement. An aluminium L-bracket (flange and web) carries a 2.0 kg avionics box mounted at the outer corners by four M4 bolts. The bracket is attached to a rigid panel (the fixed-base idealisation) by four base bolts at the underside corners: four A286 160 ksi M6 bolts in the final design, in 6.5 mm holes with 12 mm washers. The original specification and the Phase 2 gate used M5 bolts; the base bolt size was changed to M6 in Phase 2b once a joint model showed two negative M5 margins (Section 4.5). The bracket is subjected to the GEVS component qualification random spectrum: one-sided ASD 0.026 g^2/Hz at 20 and 2000 Hz, 0.16 g^2/Hz at 50 to 800 Hz, overall 14.1 Grms, applied as three independent random excitations in X, Y and Z (the launch vehicle axes), 2 minutes per axis. Two damping cases are analysed: Q = 10 (5 percent critical damping, the baseline) and Q = 20 (2.5 percent critical, the sensitivity case). All results are reported at 3-sigma (3 times the RMS value).

Baseline and requirements. Baseline bracket: web and flange both 6 mm thick, 5 mm fillet at the inner corner, no gussets. This thickness is above the brief's guidance (3 to 5 mm) but necessary for the first mode to approach 100 Hz under the declared rotary inertia of the 2 kg box. Requirement: first mode at or above 100 Hz. Margins: yield and ultimate factors per NASA-STD-5001B, 1.25 and 1.40 respectively (TRACED), applied to 3-sigma stress. The bolted joint uses the same yield and ultimate factors on bolt tension, plus a declared separation factor of 1.2 (NASA-STD-5020A, TRACED) and a slip factor of 1.25 (Section 4.5). Fatigue: Steinberg three-band method with an S-N point of 96.5 MPa at 5e8 cycles and exponent b = 6.4, allowable Miner sum 1.0.

Three phases. Phase 1 built the model, validated the chain (V1 to V6) and qualified the baseline bracket, and found it failed the first-mode requirement while leaving positive stress margins everywhere the stress was actually converged. Phase 2 stiffened the bracket (web 6 to 7 mm), demonstrated and excluded the washer-edge singularity, and checked the bolted joint with a hand prying calculation, which showed negative margins at the inner bolts. A review found Phase 2 incomplete: under the approved plan a negative margin, joint or continuum, triggers the same design-iteration clause as a failed frequency. Phase 2b (review rulings R-5 to R-14) traced the joint inputs, built a nonlinear-contact FE joint model to replace the hand prying calculation, graded the mesh convergence (V2) and an independent hand model with the root compliance (V1b), studied the box-mount footprint for mesh sensitivity (R-10), and changed the base bolts to M6 once the joint model confirmed two negative margins on the M5 design. Every band, rule and assumption across all three phases was declared in DECISIONS.md before the run it governs; none was retuned afterward.

Sources status. The GEVS table and 14.1 Grms are TRACED to GSFC-STD-7000A/B. The NASA-STD-5001B factors (1.25 yield, 1.40 ultimate) are TRACED. The 100 Hz requirement is ASSUMPTION (P7_SOURCES row 2c; the traced GEVS baseline is 50 Hz lateral, 70 Hz thrust); the 100 Hz value is industry practice for additional decoupling margin. The 3-sigma load is TRACED. The Steinberg fractions (68.27, 27.18, 4.28 percent) and exponent b = 6.4 are TRACED. Aluminium 6061-T6 properties E, nu, rho, Fty, Ftu are TRACED (minimums, Fty 240 and Ftu 290 MPa). Test duration is 2 minutes per axis, the approved plan and the GEVS qualification duration; P7_SOURCES row 1k gives 3 minutes per axis as ASSUMPTION and it is overridden by the plan (DECISIONS section 7). The bolted-joint inputs were traced in Phase 2b (P7_SOURCES.md, "R-5 joint inputs", rows 9a to 9x): sixteen TRACED, seven SECONDARY, one ASSUMPTION; the remaining ASSUMPTION items (bolt yield and shear strength for the 160 ksi A286 class, not tabulated in the traced specifications) are carried with a stated conservative bias (Section 4.5).

2. Model

Geometry. Axes: X along the web length, Y normal to the web (box on the -Y side), Z normal to the panel. Flange 120 by 80 mm, thickness tf. Web 120 by 100 mm (from the flange underside), thickness tw. Fillet 5 mm radius at the inner corner. Baseline tw = tf = 6 mm, bracket mass from the CAE volume 0.3374 kg. Final design V-A: tw = 7 mm, tf = 6 mm, mass 0.3671 kg with the M6 base holes (0.3677 kg with the original M5 holes; the larger M6 holes remove slightly more material). Increase over the baseline: 0.0297 kg (+8.8 percent of the bracket, +1.3 percent of the total supported mass).

Material. SI throughout: m, kg, s, N, Pa. g = 9.80665 m/s^2. Young modulus E = 68.9 GPa (P7_SOURCES gives 69 GPa, a 0.16 percent difference, 0.08 percent in frequency, immaterial), Poisson ratio nu = 0.33, density rho = 2700 kg/m^3. Yield strength Fty = 240 MPa (TRACED minimum), ultimate Ftu = 290 MPa (TRACED minimum).

Box idealisation. 2.0 kg point mass at the centre of gravity, located at (60, -30, 55) mm: 60 mm along the web, 30 mm out from the web face, 55 mm along the height. Rotary inertia about the CG (assumed uniform block 120 by 60 by 80 mm): Ixx = 1.667e-3 kg m^2, Iyy = 3.467e-3 kg m^2, Izz = 3.000e-3 kg m^2. Coupled to the four M4 bolt holes by a distributing coupling (Lagrange multipliers), which transmits both forces and moments but does not stiffen the web face.

Boundary condition. The four base bolt holes on the flange underside are rigidly coupled to reference nodes, which are fixed: the fixed-base idealisation, representing the panel to which the bracket is bolted. This is stiffer than a real panel with screw holes and fasteners; it is the standard qualification assumption and is stated as a licence limitation. The final design uses four A286 160 ksi M6 bolts in 6.5 mm holes with 12 mm washers (r_wash = 6.0 mm); the original specification and the Phase 2 gate used M5 bolts in 5.5 mm holes with 10 mm washers (r_wash = 5.0 mm), changed by ruling R-7 once the joint model showed two negative M5 margins (Section 4.5). The base holes are the only geometry difference between the M5 and M6 models; the web, flange and fillet are unchanged.

Random response. *RANDOM RESPONSE step, base motion excitation (*BASE MOTION, absolute acceleration), one-sided PSD input in (m/s^2)^2/Hz. Excitations X, Y and Z run as separate steps. Damping *MODAL DAMPING with viscous fraction 1/(2Q): Q = 10 corresponds to 0.05 critical damping; Q = 20 corresponds to 0.025 critical damping. Output frequency span 20 to 2000 Hz at 40 points per mode interval and bias 3 (the bracket's setting). V4 (Section 3.1) validates the engine to within 2 percent.

3-sigma convention. The random-response output PSD is integrated over frequency to give RMS (total RMS acceleration at the box, base reaction forces). The limit-load acceleration is 3 times the RMS, termed the 3-sigma value. Stress is computed from the 3-sigma input by modal superposition in post-processing, and the continuum margin of safety is stress / (Fty / 1.25) - 1 for yield, stress / (Ftu / 1.40) - 1 for ultimate.

Post-processing: true RMS von Mises and the base reaction (DECISIONS section 11). Abaqus writes RS, the RMS of each stress component, but offers no von Mises invariant for it, because the Mises of the RMS components is not the RMS of von Mises once the components lose their signs. Every von Mises number in this report is instead the true one, from the modal stress covariance: E[vm^2] = sum over mode pairs of C_kl (sigma_k^T A sigma_l), with sigma_k the mass-normalised modal stresses from the frequency step, C_kl the cross-PSD integral of the two modal transfer functions against the base ASD on a 40001-point log grid, and A the von Mises quadratic form. At every peak this true value matches Abaqus RS to within 0.6 percent. The RMS base reaction is unavailable directly, since Abaqus gives no RF or RRF in a SIM-based random response; it is instead computed by the same modal superposition and excludes modes above 3000 Hz.

3. Validation

3.1 V4, the random-response engine check against an exact SDOF integral

This is the key check: it validates the solver's random-response pipeline (*RANDOM RESPONSE, PSD definition, damping and output conventions) against an exact answer, not against another approximation. It is geometry-independent and was run once, in Phase 1, and carried unchanged through every later phase.

Method. A single-degree-of-freedom spring-mass oscillator is modelled in Abaqus: a rigid mass of 1.0 kg on a spring giving a natural frequency of 200 Hz, viscous fraction 0.05 (Q = 10), subjected to the same GEVS ASD. The exact RMS acceleration is computed by numerical integration of the transmissibility squared times the ASD over frequency (numpy, trapezoidal rule on a dense log grid).

Result. Exact absolute-acceleration RMS 22.404 g; exact relative-acceleration RMS 26.302 g, 17 percent higher, confirming the two are genuinely different quantities for this ASD. Abaqus RTA (absolute, 40 points per mode interval, bias 3) gives 22.448 g, +0.20 percent above the exact absolute integral, inside the 2 percent band. At 200 points per interval Abaqus gives 22.410 g, +0.03 percent.

Conventions settled by V4 (DECISIONS section 8). The ASD is entered as (m/s^2)^2/Hz, one-sided, per Hz, with no G parameter; *MODAL DAMPING, VISCOUS=FRACTION OF CRITICAL DAMPING is read as the true fraction of critical damping, not a percentage. RTA and not RA is the quantity to compare against Miles and against shaker control.

3.2 V1, the first mode against the closed form, and why it missed

Reference closed form. A clamped Euler-Bernoulli cantilever strip (width b = 120 mm, thickness tw, E = 68.9 GPa) of length L = 49 mm, from the top of the flange to the centroid of the box-mount holes. The tip carries the box as a rigid body: translational mass m_box + 0.2357 m_web(L), and rotary inertia J = J_cg + m_box e^2 with e = 33 mm. Stiffness K = EI/L^3 [[12, -6L], [-6L, 4L^2]]; the first root of det(K - w^2 M) = 0 is the V1 reference.

Phase 1 baseline (tw = tf = 6 mm). FE first mode 91.08 Hz. Closed form 122.08 Hz, +34.0 percent above FE. Band 10 percent, declared before any run. Verdict FAIL, bias direction as predicted.

Final design (tw = 7 mm, M5 geometry). FE first mode 101.47 Hz. Closed form re-evaluated at the new web thickness: 152.84 Hz, +50.6 percent above FE. Verdict: FAIL, and this stays in the record unchanged; V1b (Section 3.7) explains the gap but does not reclassify V1.

Why V1 fails, and why the band was not widened. The flange strip between the web root and the inner bolt row (Y = 22 mm) acts as a rotational spring at the web root, and the clamped closed form has no term for it. On the final design this root motion carries 78 percent of the total box displacement in mode 1, so a closed form that ignores it is missing most of the compliance, not a correction term. The band (10 percent) was declared before the first solve and is not retuned after a fail: the mechanism, not the number, is what gets investigated.

This comparison uses the V-A FE value on the M5 mesh (101.47 Hz), the geometry on which V1 and V1b were both graded before the base bolt was changed to M6 (Section 4.5); M6 only enlarges the rigid base footprint and does not change the web, flange or fillet that V1 and V1b model.

3.4 V3, modal effective mass

Effective mass per axis is calculated by Abaqus in the frequency step and output in the .dat file. The residual mass, modes above 2000 Hz plus the static residual, is represented by the quasi-static response in the random steps rather than by direct capture.

M5 geometry, to 2000 Hz: X 66.3 percent (mode 2 at 279.55 Hz with 0.589 kg and mode 3 at 679.17 Hz with 0.979 kg, out of 2.3677 kg total), Y 92.5 percent, Z 95.4 percent. Mode 6, at 2792.2 Hz, carries 0.668 kg of X effective mass and lies above the excitation band, acting as the residual carrier for X; retaining it raises the X capture to 94.5 percent, meeting the 90 percent band through the residual clause. Verdict PASS: Y and Z directly, X through the residual clause.

On the M6 geometry, the final design, the same check gives X 66.4 percent to 2000 Hz and 94.2 percent through the residual mode (now shifted to 2863.4 Hz with the stiffer base footprint), Y 92.5 percent, Z 95.3 percent (RESULTS_LOG.md). The mechanism is unchanged in kind; only the residual carrier's own frequency moves with the base stiffness. PASS, as before.

4. Qualification and the design iteration

4.1 The baseline fails the first-mode requirement

The Phase 1 baseline bracket (web 6 mm, flange 6 mm, no gussets) has a first mode of 91.08 Hz, below the 100 Hz requirement, a shortfall of 8.9 percent. Against the traced GEVS baseline of 50 Hz (lateral) and 70 Hz (thrust) the bracket passes comfortably. The declared requirement is 100 Hz and is binding (R-1).

R-1 sentence (from HANDOFF.md section (b), verbatim): "The baseline at 91.1 Hz fails the declared 100 Hz design requirement; it would satisfy the lower traced GEVS figures of 50 Hz (lateral) and 70 Hz (thrust)."

The 100 Hz value is ASSUMPTION in P7_SOURCES (row 2c), aerospace design practice for decoupling margin. It is kept as declared; there is no moving of the goalposts after the baseline fails.

The panel is declared rigid, not a distributed-stiffness plate: this gives the flange the stiffest possible heel support, which maximises the prying reaction at the flange edge (conservative for bolt tension), and puts all the member compliance in the flange, so the load factor is somewhat low against a real panel and the separation load correspondingly a little high (unconservative in principle, second order next to the prying geometry).

Loads. One monotonic ramp per axis and sign (Z+, Z-, Y+, Y-, X+), each followed by an unload step, carrying the friction history forward as in a test sequence. Two load levels are read on every ramp: the 3-sigma uniform-inertia (J) levels, and enveloped levels (J times the ratio by which a modal-superposition route exceeds the uniform route at the inner bolts, up to 1.85 in X). The enveloped level is the headline for the tension-driven criteria (yield, ultimate, separation); the J level is the headline for slip, since the two load routes agree on total base reaction within 1.3 percent and enveloping would inflate the interface shear unrealistically.

Criteria (DECISIONS 17.3). Bolt tension yield: MS = P_ty / P_b(1.25 L) - 1 at P_max. Ultimate: MS = P_tu / P_b(1.40 L) - 1 at P_max. Separation: MS = L_sep / (1.2 L) - 1 at P_min. Slip: MS = L_slip / (1.25 L) - 1 at P_min. If slip is negative, a shear-bearing route is evaluated (below).

Disclosure: the separation criterion changed after the first results were seen (R-11). The criterion declared first, before any joint solve, was 10 percent of the washer-annulus contact area open below 1 kPa. It was a criterion chosen for this study, not the standard's. After the M5 results were seen, a review ruled that R-6 required NASA-STD-5020's own definition, and asked for it quoted from the source before applying anything. Section 3.2.2 of NASA-STD-5020A with Change 1 defines separation as "the state of no compressive load between mating parts local to the fastener." That is full relief of the interface clamp force local to the bolt, not partial gapping of part of the footprint while compression remains elsewhere around it. The FE measure was changed accordingly to the contact normal force within r <= 10 mm (2 washer radii) of the bolt axis, graded from the same completed runs with no new solve. The 10 percent criterion's value stays in the record as a stricter secondary reading (-0.83 at Q20, M5); the NASA-STD-5020 definition is the headline everywhere below.

6. Predictions that failed

  1. V1, first-frequency closed form (declared bias: closed form higher than FE; band 10 percent). Observed: closed form +34.0 percent above FE on the baseline, +50.6 percent on the final design. What was learned: the closed form omits the flange-root rotational spring entirely, and that spring's share of the total compliance grows as the web is thickened, since the final design's root now carries 78 percent of the box motion in mode 1.
  2. The V-A frequency prediction (Section 4.2). Declared bias: "slightly low" (101.9 Hz predicted). Observed: FE 101.47 Hz, 0.4 percent below the prediction, so the bias direction was wrong; the root compliance share grew slightly with the thicker web rather than shrinking. The error was small and did not change the variant decision, but it is recorded as a bias-direction miss, not folded into "close enough."
  3. The M6 modal prediction (DECISIONS 17.8), wrong in size. Declared: f1 rises by less than 0.5 percent with M6 bolts. Observed: f1 rose from 101.47 to 104.41 Hz at 1.6 mm, +2.9 percent (mode 3 rose 7.9 percent). The larger rigid footprint at the base stiffens the root more than the prediction assumed; the direction was right, the size was not.
  4. The M5 hand prying calculation against the joint model (Section 4.5). Declared to bound bolt tension conservatively with a 5 mm lever arm and a 0.25 load factor. Observed: conservative for bolt yield at P_max (hand 2,079 N against FE 1,113 N) but unconservative at P_min (hand load factor 0.25 against FE 0.83 to 1.46, since the joint gaps locally rather than losing clamp uniformly), and far too harsh on separation and slip (hand -0.40 and -1.13 against FE > +0.17 and no slip in Y or Z). What was learned: a fixed load factor cannot represent a joint whose response depends on which side of preload it is loaded from; a nonlinear contact model was needed, not a better lever-arm guess.
  5. V5b, box RMS vs Miles on the dominant mode (declared band: within 25 percent). Observed: -33.7 to -51.1 percent, both bolt sizes. What was learned: no single mode carries the box motion in any axis; two modes of comparable effective mass share it, each contributing 0.36 to 0.60 of its own Miles value.

In every case the mechanism was investigated and reported; no band was retuned after the fact, and the 100 Hz requirement was kept exactly as declared after the 91.08 Hz baseline result (R-1). Where a wrong prediction changed the design (items 3 and 4), the design changed by the smallest declared lever, not by discarding the band.

7. Licence and limitations

Fixed-base idealisation. The washer footprints are rigid couplings, and the box is a point mass on a distributing coupling. Both are stiffer than a real panel and a real fixture; a real installation may have a first mode several percent lower than reported here.

Point-mass box. The box is a rigid point mass with declared rotary inertia. It does not model internal box modes, flexible mounting, or the real interface stiffness to the bracket.

100 Hz requirement, ASSUMPTION, P7_SOURCES row 2c. Traced GEVS figures are 50 Hz (lateral axes Y, Z) and 70 Hz (thrust axis X). The 100 Hz target is aerospace design practice for additional decoupling margin and is kept as declared under R-1.

Damping, TRACED at Q = 10 (5 percent critical), swept at Q = 20 (2.5 percent critical). The largest single uncertainty in the analysis: responses scale roughly with the square root of Q, so this is about a 41 percent swing between the two cases, in both the continuum and the joint margins.

Bolted joint model (R-6), its own idealisations (DECISIONS 17.3, 17.3a): - Panel: rigid, not a distributed-stiffness plate. Bias: maximises the prying reaction at the flange edge (conservative for bolt tension), while putting all member compliance in the flange, which makes the load factor somewhat low and the separation load slightly high against a real panel (unconservative in principle, second order next to the prying geometry). - Washers: finite and rigid, frictionless against the flange top; head friction is not counted as slip resistance, only the faying surface is. - Contact and friction: hard contact, mu = 0.20 (TRACED, uncoated clean metal, row 9b) or mu = 0.10 (TRACED, coated or chem-filmed, row 9c). Hole clearance is a square +/- 0.25 mm stop, declared, not a true circular clearance. - Mesh: 4.0 mm global with 1.5 mm local refinement, coarser than the declared 3.0 / 1.2 mm intent, for run time; no mesh convergence study was done on the joint model itself (DECISIONS 17.3a). - Inputs, traced against assumed (R-5, Section 4.5): TRACED: Ftu, A_t, preload scatter, mu (both values), the separation factor and definition, K. ASSUMPTION: Fty (0.70 Ftu, conservative against Phase 2's 827 MPa hand value), Fsu (0.60 Ftu), the 5 percent relaxation allowance (conservative for the clamp), the slip factor 1.25 (conservative against 1.0), bolt modulus 201 GPa. SECONDARY: the bearing allowables (207 / 345 MPa, conservative against the recalled Phase 2 values). - The d = 3.0 mm continuum rule is applied to the M6 base footprint without a new singularity study (Section 4.4); the design point sits on the edge of that zone. - The M6 X+ separation margin rests on the M5 run as a bound, not a direct M6 result (Section 4.5).

Fatigue. The 4x life factor is ASSUMPTION (row 3e). The S-N point (96.5 MPa at 5e8 cycles) is the brief's; P7_SOURCES row 6g gives a SECONDARY range of 70 to 90 MPa, and the 70 MPa end is carried as a conservative sensitivity (Section 4.6).

No test correlation. Every number in this report is a model result: traced material properties, a declared damping assumption, declared idealisations, and, for the joint, a declared rigid panel and idealised hole-clearance stops. No shaker test, on this bracket or a comparable one, has been run to check the fixed-base assumption, the real Q of the assembly, or the joint behaviour, including the P_min gross-slip regime, against these predictions.

8. What I would do next

  1. Test correlation. No shaker test exists for this bracket, panel or joint. A prototype qualification test would check the fixed-base idealisation, the real Q of the assembly, and, most importantly, whether the joint model's rigid panel and idealised hole-clearance stops predict the real bolt and clamp behaviour under 3-sigma random loads.
  2. A preload-controlled installation. The bolt tension yield margin is thin (+0.024) and the +/- 35 percent torque-control scatter uses almost all of it (Section 4.5). Specifying lubrication-controlled torque (+/- 25 percent, TRACED) or a lower installation torque, and re-checking yield, separation and slip at both preload bounds, would open this margin without a further geometry change.
  3. The coated-surface case. Slip is positive at the declared mu = 0.20 but would be negative at mu = 0.10 (anodised or chem-filmed faying surfaces). If the flight hardware's actual surface finish is not yet fixed, the shear-bearing route (already positive, Section 4.5) should be carried forward as the qualifying route rather than assumed away, or the surface finish should be specified and traced.
  4. The P_min gross-slip regime in X+. P7_J6MIN was stopped mid-ramp in an unrestrained gross-slip regime after the graded quantities were already written; it was not run to completion, and the M6 X+ separation margin currently rests on the M5 run as a bound (Section 4.5). Finishing that run, or rerunning it with tighter increment control through the slip transition, would replace that bound with a direct M6 result.
  5. Mass reduction direction, if a future iteration allows it. With the first mode now passing by more margin (104.41 Hz on M6, a 4.4 percent margin) than the M5 design's 1.5 percent, and every continuum and joint margin positive, there may be room to trim mass; any such pass would need to re-check the joint margins, not just the frequency, since the M6 fix was driven by the joint, not by stiffness.
